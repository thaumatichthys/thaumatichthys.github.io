Make fabry perot antenna. Reasoning: Conventional patch array has long feedlines, FR4 have high loss at 6 GHz, feedline = bad. Fabry perot = no need for long feedlines since there can be just a few driven patch elements and the rest is done by the metamaterial (!!!) PRS.

First I experimented with the unit cell. For the partially reflective surface (PRS), the unit cells should ideally be less than one wavelength across to reduce the effect of grating lobes. In my case I am designing the antenna for a 200 MHz bandwidth from 5.7 to 5.9 GHz, so a wavelength of around 52mm. In my case I kind of arbitrarily decided to make the unit cells 20mm across.

First I tried a kind of double split-ring shape. This one worked somewhat, but an issue I had with it was that it had somewhat messy resonant poles and its phase response was kind of weird.

I then tried a simple square frame shape. This one had a really smooth phase and magnitude response for S11, and it appeared to have high Q as well. In the case of the PRS I actually don't want a perfect resonance, but in this case the magnitude of S11 ramped so smoothly that I thought I could simply detune it for the frequency of interest, and just account for the small phase shift.

For a fabry perot antenna, the gain is a function of the PRS's reflectivity.

\( G = \frac{1 + R}{1 - R} \)

baiscally i use ring of 14mm OD, 12mm ID, unit cell pitch = 20mm.
